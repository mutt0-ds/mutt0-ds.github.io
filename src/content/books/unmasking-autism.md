## Notes 

- Autism is often diagnosed late and is affected by many biases since we focus on a certain stereotype. It often goes undiagnosed and people mask it.
- Autistic people's neurons fire readily but do not distinguish the importance of input (e.g., street noise vs someone crying).
- They focus on facial features and individual details rather than the whole, so they struggle to recognize faces and expressions.
- They analyze the world systematically and analytically; others do it more deductively.
- Classic middle-school masking: pretending not to care about school and acting tough to fit in, and not grasping social norms.
- Two types of behavior: camouflage to blend in with others, and compensation as shortcuts to get around disability-related difficulties.
- The idea of high functioning is flawed. Someone who excels at work might be hiding that they have not socialized in months. It is a facade.
- Sensory overload that can be masked (but it is draining) or a meltdown.
- Alcohol use and autism are often linked in the sense that it can be a way to loosen up and feel accepted. Important to understand it is not a solution.
- Autistic people are often hypersensitive, but may not feel pain. Alexithymia: difficulty describing their emotions.
- They also tend to be accommodating and to always say yes to please everyone. Learn to say no if a condition is not acceptable.
- Society must change to allow autistic people to unmask without difficulty.
- Do not be in egg mode. That feeling of being wrong, not yourself. Something is not right.
