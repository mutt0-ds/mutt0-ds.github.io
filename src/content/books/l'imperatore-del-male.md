It won a Pulitzer, well written. The topic will be tough for me, but I want to learn more.

Hard, very hard to read. And truly an excellent book, very detailed, that explores the history of cancer. I feel I have learned a lot about this illness, that it is made of deranged cells that multiply and are incredibly hard to stop, let alone prevent. But many, many people have committed and are working to confront this terrible disease. Harrowing stories, but also stories of real hope and people who made it. It feels a bit less invincible. A 672-page read, but what a read.

## Notes

- We start with Galen and his theory that tumors were a black fluid invading the body. 
- It then evolves with the radical mastectomy, practically removing large parts of the patient's body to eradicate the tumor. It worked only at an early stage and was brutal.
- Cancer is heterogeneous. It presents in various forms and each of them responds differently to treatments. This means there is no single medicine but different approaches.
- Brilliant chapter on the fight against smoking!
- Chemo: Cancer therapy is like hitting the dog with a stick to get the fleas off.
- Final finding: cancer is caused by mutations in certain genes. Some factors like smoking increase the probability that this happens.
- A couple of memorable quotes:
